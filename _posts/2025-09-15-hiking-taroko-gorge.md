Located on Taiwan's rugged eastern coastline in Hualien County, **Taroko National Park** is one of the premier natural wonders of Asia. Carved over millions of years by tectonic uplift and the erosive power of the Liwu River, its sheer marble canyons rise hundreds of meters above turquoise riverbeds.

![Taroko Gorge Mountain Peaks]({{ site.github.url }}/assets/img/mountains.jpg)

## Top Trails for Every Hiking Level

### 1. Shakadang Trail (砂卡礑步道) — Easy & Scenic
Carved into cliff faces alongside the crystal-clear, azure Shakadang Stream, this flat 4.5 km walk leads through traditional Truku indigenous settlements. Look down into the water to see brilliant turquoise pools and smooth rock formations.

* **Length**: ~4.5 km round-trip
* **Difficulty**: Easy / Family-friendly
* **Highlight**: Shaded canyon path with indigenous food stalls at the midway point.

### 2. Swallow Grotto (燕子口) — Dramatic Marble Caverns
Here, the canyon narrows to just a few dozen meters. Walking along the protected road footpath, you can look across the gorge at thousands of natural potholes carved into marble walls where Pacific swallows nest.

* **Safety Tip**: Wear the free safety helmets distributed at the trail entrance due to potential falling rocks.

### 3. Baiyang Trail & Water Curtain Cave (白楊步道)
Passing through seven tunnels carved through the rock, Baiyang Trail opens into expansive valley views and ends at the famed **Water Curtain Cave**, where spring water pours down like torrential rain from the cave ceiling.

* **Gear**: Bring a reliable headlamp/flashlight and a waterproof rain poncho!

## How to Get There from Taipei

* **Train**: Catch the TRA Tze-Chiang or Puyuma express train from Taipei Main Station to **Hualien Station** (approx. 2 hours).
* **Local Transit**: From Hualien Station, hire a licensed taxi driver for the day, rent a scooter (if you have an International Driving Permit), or take the Taroko Tourist Shuttle Bus (Taiwan Tourist Shuttle Route 310).
